- Audit: 5/5 pass. Random draw 0.3256 → auto-publish eligible.

**TLDR:** Supervisors voted 9-2 to cut the affordable share market-rate developers must build from 15 percent to 5 percent. Developments under 24 units are now exempt entirely.

**Editor's note:** San Francisco has now cut its inclusionary rate twice in three years, and both times the cut came with a promise that the number climbs back when the market recovers. In 2023, Aaron Peskin and London Breed dropped the onsite requirement from 22 percent to between 12 and 15 percent, sold as a housing stimulus. That reset never came. Myrna Melgar said Tuesday that this one is a temporary moment and she expects a future board to raise it. Set that against what Controller Greg Wagner actually wrote in April: requirements significantly above zero would further threaten feasibility and would not create additional affordable housing. The ratchet turns one direction, and each cut makes the next one easier to justify. Jackie Fielder's 8 percent Mission carve-out passed 6-5, which means five supervisors were ready to end neighborhood-specific rates entirely.

**What to watch:** Melgar's affordable housing fund, which would grow to $125 million a year, goes to voters in November. The new rates expire in three years.

## 2. AB 1482 exemption and the 64% hike (HOUSING, first-to-connect)

- Source: SF Standard, Jennifer Wadsworth, 2026-07-15
- URL: https://sfstandard.com/2026/07/15/sf-rent-hike-family-displaced-gantry-mill-creek-ab-1482-loophole/
- composite 9.05 / uniqueness 9
- Audit: 5/5 pass. Random draw 0.8528 → auto-publish eligible.

**TLDR:** A Dogpatch family's rent at The Gantry jumped 64 percent to $5,002 because their 2014 building is exempt from state rent caps. More than 14,000 SF apartments share that exemption.

**Editor's note:** On the same day supervisors voted to cut affordability requirements because building is too hard, this is what the last boom's buildings are doing to the people inside them. AB 1482 exempts any building under 15 years old, a carve-out written to keep developers building. San Francisco produced roughly 14,000 exempt units between 2011 and 2020, and they cluster in SoMa, Mission Bay, the Financial District, and Dogpatch. The exemption expires building by building, which hands landlords a countdown and an incentive: clear out the below-market tenants before the cap lands. David Chiu wrote AB 1482 as an assemblymember. He is now the city attorney watching his own law's exemption produce the increases he wrote it to stop. Production policy and displacement policy are the same policy, and only one of them got a vote Tuesday.

**What to watch:** The Gantry ages into AB 1482 coverage around 2029. Roughly 2,600 more San Francisco units cross the 15-year threshold within three years.

**Note:** this is the strongest item of the day. The connection between the Tuesday inclusionary vote and the Standard's Wednesday investigation is not made by either outlet.
