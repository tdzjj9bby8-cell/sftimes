      A rent freeze is available to San Francisco today, and the tenant advocates who would seem likeliest to want one do not. Amending Chapter 37 to set the maximum annual increase at zero would take a simple majority of the Board of Supervisors. Nobody is asking. “We're very cautious anytime we fiddle with the rent ordinance,” said Meg Heisler of the San Francisco Anti-Displacement Coalition, who worries an amendment invites landlord lawsuits. The deeper reason is arithmetic. The current cap on annual increases is 1.6 percent. A freeze touches the small number and leaves the large one alone.

      San Jose is moving to ban something its councilmembers say is not currently happening, which is the most interesting thing about it. The Rules and Open Government Committee voted unanimously Wednesday to have the city manager draft a policy letting officials review proprietary information without signing nondisclosure agreements. Councilmember Peter Ortiz, a lead author, says he knows of no sitting member who has signed one for a data center. The precedent is behind them: nearly 20 city officials, including then-Mayor Sam Liccardo, signed NDAs in 2017 at Google's request. San Jose has roughly 20 data centers running, six under construction and five awaiting approval.
